## 1. General ability and specialized models

I am very doubtful about so-called artificial general intelligence. First, define it. Is the aim to replace humans, have very broad abilities, simply become powerful, or evolve by itself?

AI is already stronger than humans in some areas and has replaced parts of some jobs. If "general" means broad ability, it is not necessary at all. A prototype can be very general, but a model doing real work must specialize in one area, or its cost is too high. Current large language models basically work this way. They first identify the content, then route it to expert models. More precisely, expert models activate only some parameters. Simple questions get less thought, while complex cases use a stronger mode. Every product must consider cost if it is to be used in practice.

## 2. Self-evolution, productivity, and mistakes

I think evolution is neither possible nor something that should happen. Has human thinking evolved? I think it has hardly changed. Small adjustments and improvements have always been made. Another kind of evolution is learning special tasks, but that is unnecessary too. A model can record its mistakes and be reminded through context.

For productivity, I think we may already be at the highest point. It is hard to imagine what new things will appear, and the field is already very crowded. AI videos are everywhere, but there are only so many hours in a day. What is produced now is consumer content. I have not seen AI create things that bring much new productive power. Although I do not read The Three-Body Problem, I think AI may be a sophon used to block human technology.

Mistakes have an element of probability. Human mistakes are not really mistakes, but taking the wrong path. This cannot be prevented in advance, so AI will also make mistakes. Physics determines this. No matter how many parameters there are, they cannot predict random numbers. Simply put, it is like a university graduate lacking work experience. More practice is still needed.

## 3. The current approach and its limits

The current approach uses large language models, or LLMs, to achieve general intelligence. This approach may be wrong. There are certainly many ways to build intelligence, but this one is completely different from a human brain. I think its mathematical structure may be flawed. Of course, humans also have evolutionary inertia, and biological evolution may not be the best choice.

There is not much high-quality text left. Much data is dirty and not original enough, which may cause training to collapse. Cost also matters. Electricity and chips are limited. Intelligence previously appeared with 100 billion parameters. But if the next level needs 1 quadrillion parameters, both training and use will have astronomical costs.

Current large models still lack xxx. They do not know what text they output. Humans actually give meaning to much of that text. They lack an internal thinking mechanism, or it is very weak. They can only think by talking. This is not completely impossible, but it is naturally weaker. In simple terms, they predict but do not understand. Knowing does not mean being able to describe.

Engineering improvements may now be at their limit, and fine-tuning may be near its limit too. The current level should be the highest performance. Large language models have two important directions. One is chains of thought: keep outputting, then show users the last small part. The other is expert models, which analyze specific matters more closely. They judge what users want, whether they are asking for knowledge or doing work, and what kind of knowledge is involved. This provides routing.

## 4. Social effects and use cases

AI now disrupts production too much and may create a gap between generations of designers. There is also a special situation: lower-level AI already on the market may prevent higher-level AI from appearing, because they compete for the same market. More advanced AI is harder to analyze and manage, and may be more dangerous.

What we need may only be simpler helper machines, such as automatic cooking machines, washing machines, and coffee machines. General intelligence also needs real use cases.

Current large models lack the most important ability: system design. Without it, they cannot design more advanced AI. They need to predict how something can be put into practice, how chips should be designed, and what code is needed. This requires strong overall planning.

There may not be enough time either. AI training needs huge amounts of electricity and other resources, and is very fragile. It cannot emerge in chaotic conditions. A major misunderstanding is that AI has strongly affected society, so future effects must be even larger. That is wrong. Many jobs, especially paperwork, are already easy to replace. Text-based, rule-based work is the most suitable for automation. Even without large models, advanced programs could replace it. This has been happening all along.

AI reading aloud and translation existed five years ago. The main problem then was poor CPU performance, and these tasks are suitable for batch processing. More physical abilities, such as image recognition, do not necessarily use large models. More precisely, they are not text-based large models, even if they can output text.

## 5. Programming automation and possible improvements

AI programming also needs detailed discussion. Code itself is a process of improving architecture and increasing automation. The history of programming languages shows this well: from machine instructions to higher-level languages and then more modern ones, automation keeps increasing. Even without AI, more automated, modular, and modern programming languages would appear.

Overall, I think there will be no more progress. Existing technology may not even remain available at current prices. This is not a question of being pessimistic. What I see leads me to this judgment. I do not know what the future will be, and I do not want to know anymore.

Finally, some possible improvements. First, recognize that human language is flawed. A classic programmer joke says, "Buy two tomatoes. If there are potatoes, buy three." What does "three" refer to? The reference is clearly ambiguous. Simply put, human language lacks brackets, and large language models have this problem too.

There should also be modifiers. "Computer parts" in "I want computer parts" may have another hidden meaning. This should be designed at the token level. Common words can use different tokens inside a model. If a word has several meanings, internal thinking should use meanings the model can clearly distinguish, while outside input and output still use the ambiguous words of natural language.
