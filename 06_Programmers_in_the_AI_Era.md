## 1. Competition and ability

First, I need to say that I do not have a good opinion of programmers as a group. You know best what you have done. I will not go into that here.

Competition for a place in the market will become stronger, because only a few things will really make a difference. For example, suppose we make a smart TV system. It can run on almost any TV-like device. If your system cannot meet its standard, it will be pushed out. Manufacturers and customers will choose the best software. Requirements may be as strict as the number of seconds needed to start. So research and learning are still necessary. You need not only to write code, but also to understand hardware and electronics and avoid possible problems.

## 2. Work and the hardware and software ecosystem

Work may move from mainly writing code to finding more potential or studying use cases in depth, trying to make things as ergonomic as possible. In other words, you can legitimately watch videos while slacking off at work. The job will be closer to sales than to a worker's role.

Software may also become more closely tied to hardware. Without hardware-based encryption protection, software may be stolen. If it is stolen, you cannot earn money. So we may return to an era of frequent hardware replacement. However, open-source software is also a strong competitor.

Platforms may need stronger and better care for their ecosystems. There may be many companies like Apple, handling everything from hardware and software development kits to the software ecosystem, and providing special services.

## 3. Large models and development tools

There will be prompt engineers, or engineers with rich experience working with large models. AI can now write good code, but it still cannot make very good products. It needs human guidance. Humans really exist in the real world. Large models are powerful, but they are still machines.

Editors may appear that place strict limits on large models. For example, we may agree not to use C++ exceptions, but a model may forget this after its context is compressed. The editor must enforce the rule. String parsing can also happen on the CPU instead of the GPU. There may be other limits, such as requiring "xxx."

## 4. Work schedules

Another painful possibility is more frequent overtime. Companies may demand faster production and product updates, leaving very little sleep. You may give a model heavy or more independent tasks while you sleep, but need to wake up during the process. Sleep may be split into about three periods.

Worse, you may need to manage several large models working on several tasks at once. A model may take about half an hour per task. You can do other things in that time, so you may have to supervise several unrelated projects.

The job may no longer be just entering prompts and adjusting them. You may need to manage each agent separately and make them work together. That increases the difficulty by an order of magnitude.
