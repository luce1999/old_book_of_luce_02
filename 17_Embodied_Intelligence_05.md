## 1. Why smart driving succeeds more easily

Next, why can smart driving succeed while robots are less successful? One simple reason is that smart driving only handles a two-dimensional plane. There are only a few factors, such as forward, backward, left, right, acceleration, and slowing down. The car only needs to move forward. Robots have much more to consider: how to move their limbs, whether they xxx, whether they hit other things, and whether they trip.

There is also plenty of car data, and many human-driven cars already exist. This reduces the difficulty by an order of magnitude. Cars can have more sensors. Their safety goals are also fairly simple: do not hit other vehicles, do not hit pedestrians, and do not run red lights.

## 2. Smart driving and traffic

Self-driving cars should become more common. Driving still takes a lot of mental effort. There may be more small buses and even smaller electric cars for just one person, similar to today's three-wheelers.

I do not think more smart-driving vehicles will improve traffic. Vehicles differ between manufacturers, and smart driving was not designed to cooperate with other vehicles. Cooperation would be very troublesome and unnecessary. The only potentially useful approach is to force vehicles onto different routes, making some take a longer way to reduce congestion.

## 3. Roles of humans and robots

My overall judgment is that humans may still lead in the long run. Humans make the main decisions, and robots do the work. Even fully independent robots will have human employees watching in the background. Humans will continue providing the final-mile guarantee.

Large machines with fixed functions may become automatic. Small machines close to daily life may still need partial automation or very good ergonomic design.
