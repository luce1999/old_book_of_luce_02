## 1. Robot safety

Even a small robot has a physical body in the real world. This differs from internet content, which is mainly video and audio and can cause limited harm. As a remotely controlled computer, it must have strict security. Security must be built on existing computer frameworks, such as TLS 1.3.

It is best to have an emergency shutdown option somewhere like the back. There must also be remote shutdown, because a robot acting wildly may be impossible to approach. The robot itself also needs safety protection. If contact with the outside is lost for about 10 seconds, it should stop. It also needs prediction and 3D modeling inside. If it thinks the current action is dangerous, it should immediately shut down and report it, except for special models.

Some kind of chain may also be needed, or special robots designed to stop other robots. Nets or ropes could also make a robot tangle itself up.

## 2. Information and everyday risks

Robots also have the problem of sending information out. Returning them to the factory for repair, or making decisions outside the robot itself, may leak information.

Many robots already exist, such as vending machines. They simply have no arms or legs. So it is doubtful whether we need large numbers of robots. Their hands can make all kinds of things happen. They can open office doors, secretly take things, or unplug cables. What humans can do, robots can do too. This creates serious safety risks.

Robots are loud and unnatural when moving, which affects people's mood at work. Standing up to use the toilet or get a glass of water is itself part of relaxing. I think robot help is often unnecessary. Frankly, robots are factory arms, and very dangerous.

## 3. More practical uses

What we may need most now is a robot that carries things upstairs. It may need a walking mechanism to fit current buildings. This work uses a lot of physical effort. Outside, people can use vehicles and electric bikes, perhaps also for transport within residential communities. I think this has the greatest business potential. But development would be very hard because the environment is complex. I also think there may not be enough money to buy the robots.

If I must choose, I think sliding tracks on outside walls or small lifts would be better.
